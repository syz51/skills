# Editorial review

Use this guide for substantial revisions, manuscript or article reviews, and requested reader-comprehension checks. Choose only the checks that address the actual task; there is no mandatory number of passes, alternatives, compliments, or iterations.

## Review the text that exists

- Establish the requested edit depth and intended reader. Read enough surrounding material to judge the changed section, but do not invent problems in unseen text or expand a paragraph review into a whole-project rewrite.
- Check argument and structure: what the piece asks the reader to understand, whether the evidence supports it, and whether sections contribute in a useful order. Flag contradictions and missing context before polishing sentences.
- Check expression and voice: unclear references, ambiguous claims, unnecessary repetition, inconsistent terminology, and changes in register. For procedural explanations, clarify inputs and outputs, what changes or stays fixed, and how stages connect. Preserve deliberate style and distinctions that matter to the author or subject.
- Check evidence separately from fluency. For material assertions, use the research guide to assess support; preserve reported experience and flag unsupported strengthening introduced by an edit. Copyediting cannot establish a study's quality or a product's performance.
- Check mechanics within scope: grammar, punctuation, headings, link labels, citation consistency, and requested length. Avoid silently rewriting quotations or changing technical identifiers.

For review-only work, report the material findings with locations, reasons, and proposed corrections. For authorized edits, apply the corrections and inspect what changed. Keep unaffected text and facts stable; identify any consequential change in meaning. Strengths are useful when they explain what to preserve, not as compulsory praise.

## Check reader comprehension when useful

For a substantial document or a user-requested test, ask whether a reader can recover the intended conclusion, supporting reasons, next action if any, and important limitations from the document alone.

- An independent reader or subagent may help when available and authorized. Give it the document and realistic reader questions, without supplying the intended answers or the author's hidden context. Honor confidentiality and execution boundaries.
- Compare its answers with the actual document and supporting evidence. Identify missing definitions, unjustified assumptions, ambiguity, or contradictions, then correct demonstrated problems within the authorized scope.
- Treat model feedback as a diagnostic aid, not observed human comprehension, an expert panel, a calibrated quality score, or proof of factual truth. If no independent test was performed, describe a self-review accurately.
- Stop when the requested checks pass and further iterations are unlikely to change a material finding. Do not require repeated sign-off or endless revision for its own sake.
